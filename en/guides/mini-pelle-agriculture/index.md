# Agricultural Mini Excavator: Uses on the Farm

Clearing ditches, installing fencing, drainage, maintaining tracks or digging pits: on the farm, the mini excavator has become a versatile tool that advantageously replaces many manual or subcontracted jobs. Here's how to make the most of it, what tonnage to aim for and which attachments to prioritise.

On a farm, earthmoving and maintenance work is constant: a ditch silting up, a fence to repair, a track washed out by rain, a pit to dig for a drinking trough or a tank. For a long time, these jobs relied on the spade and pickaxe, or on the occasional visit of a contractor. The agricultural mini excavator has changed all that: compact, economical and versatile, it lets you stay self-sufficient on most jobs.
This guide reviews the practical uses on the farm, the tonnage to favour depending on your work, the attachments that make a difference, and how to invest without breaking the bank thanks to direct import.

## Why a mini excavator on the farm
The main benefit of a mini excavator in farming is self-sufficiency . Rather than waiting for a contractor to become available or hiring a machine on a job-by-job basis, you can act at the right moment, often out of season or between two crop tasks. A ditch gets cleared when it needs to be, not when the contractor happens to pass by.
The second advantage is controlled cost . On a farm, earthmoving needs come round every year. Over time, cumulative hire or subcontracting adds up significantly. A machine written off over several seasons quickly becomes profitable, especially as a new mini excavator starts from €4,125 excl. VAT with us. To compare the two approaches, our guide on [buying or hiring a mini excavator](/en/guides/mini-pelle-acheter-ou-louer/) details the break-even thresholds.
Finally, the mini excavator is versatile . With a few attachments, a single machine digs, clears, levels, drills post holes or handles loads. It's a Swiss army knife that finds its place alongside the tractor, without competing with it.

## The main agricultural uses
Here are the most common jobs where a mini excavator proves its worth on the farm.

### Ditch clearing and maintenance
Drainage ditches silt up with sediment, leaves and vegetation. Regular clearing keeps water flowing and protects fields from waterlogging. A mini excavator fitted with a grading bucket (wide and shallow) does this job cleanly, respecting the drainage gradient. It's a recurring use that, on its own, often justifies the purchase.

### Installing and repairing fencing
Digging post holes with a crowbar is exhausting. With an auger mounted on a mini excavator, you can drill even, regular holes quickly, even in hard or stony ground. The machine also serves to level the fence line and backfill neatly. For long runs of pasture fencing, the time saved is considerable.

### Drainage and buried networks
Laying a drain, a water pipe to a trough, an electrical supply to a building or an irrigation network means digging even trenches. The mini excavator excels at this kind of work, as our dedicated guide on [trenches and networks](/en/guides/mini-pelle-tranchee-reseaux/) explains. A narrow bucket produces clean cuts that limit the amount of backfill to replace.

### Maintaining tracks and farmyards
Farm tracks suffer from the passage of machinery and from run-off. The mini excavator reprofiles the surface, re-cuts the drainage channels, fills potholes and spreads material with the blade or a grading bucket. It also makes it easier to maintain access to buildings and lay out yards.

### Digging pits and small structures
A pit for a buried tank, a silo, a water reserve, the foundation of a shelter, the slab of a technical room: all these small structures benefit from the precise earthmoving of a mini excavator. On more technical jobs such as a pond, our article on [earthmoving with a mini excavator](/en/guides/mini-pelle-terrassement/) gives the best practices.

## Which tonnage to choose
The choice of tonnage depends on the nature of your work, the space available and transport. Here are the main categories useful on the farm.
Size Typical agricultural uses Points to watch
Micro excavator (~1 t) Small ditches, pipe trenches, narrow access, yard Limited reach and depth, very manoeuvrable
Mini excavator 1.5 to 2 t Clearing, drainage, post holes, tracks The best versatility/transport compromise
Mini excavator 2.5 to 3.5 t Pits, heavy clearing, structures, backfilling More power, transport to plan ahead

For most farms, a [1.5 to 2 tonne mini excavator](/en/guides/mini-pelle-1-5-tonne-guide/) covers the bulk of needs while remaining easy to transport behind a pick-up or tractor. If your jobs are occasional and light, a [micro excavator](/en/guides/micro-pelle-tout-savoir/) may suffice. If in doubt, our method for [choosing a mini excavator](/en/guides/comment-choisir-mini-pelle/) guides you step by step according to your profile.
Also think about transport : a 1.5 tonne machine can be moved on a light trailer, whereas above that you need a more robust trailer. Our [plant trailers](/en/remorques/) come in 1.5 t and 2.7 t versions, and the guide on [transport on a trailer](/en/guides/transport-mini-pelle-remorque/) sets out the GVW and lashing rules.

## Useful attachments
The versatility of an agricultural mini excavator owes a lot to its attachments. Here are the ones that come up most often on a farm:
- Wide grading bucket: essential for ditches and fine levelling, it moves large volumes of loose material.
- Narrow buckets (30 to 40 cm): perfect for drainage and network trenches.
- Auger: for fence post holes and planting.
- Grapple or sorting grab: for handling stones, stumps, timber or backfill.
- Hydraulic breaker: useful in rocky ground or for demolishing small structures.
- Blade and quick hitches: the blade stabilises the machine and is used for reprofiling, while the quick hitch speeds up tool changes.
A well-designed quick hitch lets you switch from a bucket to an auger in a few minutes, which changes everything on a varied working day.
